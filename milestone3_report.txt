HashMap Operations (Put and Get)

The HashMap has an average time complexity of O(1) for both put and get operations. 
To handle collisions, separate chaining is used. 
To keep performance sustained, a load factor threshold of 0.8 is used. 
When the map reaches 80% capacity, a rehash operation doubles the table size, ensuring that average chain lengths remain small. 
While the theoretical worst-case is O(n) if all keys hash to a single index, regular rehashing makes this unlikely.

Sorting Algorithms Complexity

Two sorting algorithms are implemented:

Merge Sort: This algorithm provides a guaranteed time complexity of O(nlogn). 
It recursively splitting the roster into halves and merging them back in sorted order.

Quick Sort: This algorithm offers an average-case complexity of O(nlogn). 
However, its worst-case complexity is O(n²), which can occur if the pivot selection picks the smallest or largest elements, such as when sorting already-sorted data.

Algorithm Comparison and Preference

While both algorithms share an average O(n log n) runtime, they serve different needs:

Merge Sort is preferred when stability is required. 
It ensures that records with identical keys maintain their original relative order. 
Because its performance never drops to O(n²), it is the most reliable choice.

Quick Sort is often preferred for general in-memory applications because it typically has lower constant factors and better cache performance, making it faster in practice on average data.

For this system, Merge Sort is the recommended choice. Its complexity of O(nlogn) ensures consistent results and prevising the order of students.
